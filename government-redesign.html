Confidential public-sector project

Government Services Platform

Redesigning how citizens understand and complete complex government procedures.

My Role

Connecting structure, content and interaction decisions.

My responsibilities covered the product design work needed to clarify the procedure experience.

  • Information Architecture
  • UX Research synthesis
  • User Flows
  • Interaction Design
  • UX Writing
  • High Fidelity Prototypes
  • Cross-functional collaboration

Overview

The existing experience made procedures feel harder than they were.

Citizens entered procedures without understanding requirements or expected outcomes. Important information appeared too late, and dense, inconsistent interfaces meant abandonment came from high cognitive load, not a navigation problem.

This redesign aimed to simplify procedures by helping citizens understand a process before starting it — reducing confusion and creating a scalable experience for future digital government services.

Process & Design System

A structured journey built for citizen comprehension.

Mapping the citizen experience from discovery to completion as a continuous, low-friction diagram, with screen structures standardized across distinct government procedures to ensure instant familiarity.

Inform before asking

Citizens should understand a procedure before deciding to start it. The redesigned flow introduces requirements, expected steps and outcomes before asking users to commit. This information is visible even without signing in, so citizens can evaluate a procedure before creating an account.

Reduce cognitive load

Instead of showing everything on one screen, the experience was broken into smaller focused steps. More screens can feel simpler when each one carries a clear purpose.

Contextual help

Support should appear when users need it. Instead of repeating documentation, guidance adapts to the current step and helps citizens move forward with confidence.

A conclusion along the way

Reducing clicks does not necessarily reduce complexity.

One discovery changed the direction of the redesign: the experience did not need to be shorter at any cost, it needed to feel easier to understand. Reducing cognitive load became the design goal — in some cases, adding an extra screen created a simpler experience, because each step could focus on one decision at a time. Less effort was never about fewer clicks. It was about clearer decisions.

Before vs After

From dense procedure pages to guided comprehension.

The redesign shifted the experience from presenting everything at once to helping citizens understand the process progressively.

Before: Missing Context — A separate legacy screen offered only a plate selector and a list of conditions and required documents, with no fee information before starting.
Before: Everything at Once — The full procedure form displayed every applicant field, document upload and progress metric on one long scroll, with no separation between steps.

Outcome

Designed for implementation and future scale.

The project is still under implementation, so this case study does not claim metrics or business impact.

The intended impact is to reduce cognitive load, improve comprehension, encourage adoption of digital government services and create a scalable framework for future procedures. Every government procedure is different — the interaction pattern should not be. A reusable framework improves learnability across procedures as the platform scales.
