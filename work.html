Overview

Helping citizens understand before they start.

This platform redesign aimed to simplify complex government procedures by helping citizens understand a process before starting it.

The work focused on reducing confusion, improving comprehension and creating a scalable experience for future digital government procedures.

The Challenge

The existing experience made procedures feel harder than they were.

Citizens entered procedures without fully understanding requirements, steps or expected outcomes. Important information appeared too late, after users had already committed to a flow.

Interfaces contained excessive information, technical language and inconsistent interaction patterns. Product analytics also showed abandonment in key moments, pointing to high cognitive load rather than a simple navigation problem.

Before vs After

From dense procedure pages to guided comprehension.

The redesign shifted the experience from presenting everything at once to helping citizens understand the process progressively.

Understanding the Problem

Reducing clicks does not necessarily reduce complexity.

One discovery changed the direction of the redesign: the experience did not need to be shorter at any cost. It needed to feel easier to understand.

Reducing cognitive load became the design goal. In some cases, adding an extra screen created a simpler experience because each step could focus on one decision at a time.

Less effort was not about fewer clicks. It was about clearer decisions.

Design Decisions

Four decisions shaped the redesign.

The design system had to support different government procedures without making each flow feel like a new product.

Inform before asking

Citizens should understand a procedure before deciding to start it. The redesigned flow introduces requirements, expected steps and outcomes before asking users to commit.

Reduce cognitive load

Instead of showing everything on one screen, the experience was broken into smaller focused steps. More screens can feel simpler when each one carries a clear purpose.

Standardize the interaction model

Every government procedure is different. The interaction pattern should not be. A reusable framework improves learnability across procedures.

Contextual help

Support should appear when users need it. Instead of repeating documentation, guidance adapts to the current step and helps citizens move forward with confidence.

User Journey

A clearer path through digital government procedures.

The journey was organized as a sequence of comprehension and action, not a collection of disconnected screens.

  1. Browse procedures

  2. Learn about the procedure

  3. Sign in

  4. Complete guided flow

  5. Submit documents

  6. Finish

Outcome

Designed for implementation and future scale.

The project is still under implementation, so this case study does not claim metrics or business impact.

The intended impact is to reduce cognitive load, improve comprehension, encourage adoption of digital government services and create a scalable framework for future procedures.

Reflection

Reducing clicks is not the same as reducing effort.

This project reinforced that simplicity is not always about shortening the path. Sometimes an extra screen creates a significantly simpler experience.

The most valuable design decisions were the ones that made the procedure easier to understand before asking citizens to act.
